Data Analysis Panel

This panel loads the participant records collected by the study (no manual export needed) and visualises three research hypotheses: H1 Portrait vs landscape, H2 Colour vs grayscale, H3 Self-estimate vs actual accuracy, together with cross-tabulated statistics by group / authenticity, and one-click export of the merged detail data.

Self-estimate bias = self-estimated number correct − actual number correct. A positive value means the participant overestimated themselves (overconfidence).

H1 · Image category

Portrait vs Landscape

Hypothesis: people are better at detecting AI-generated portraits than landscapes. Bar heights are the mean accuracy (%) of each group; n is the number of judgments in that group.

H2 · Presentation mode

Colour vs Grayscale

Hypothesis: people classify colour images more accurately than grayscale ones. Bar heights are the mean accuracy (%) of each group; n is the number of judgments in that group.

H3 · Self-estimate vs Actual

Metacognitive bias

Hypothesis: participants tend to overestimate their own ability. Each point is one participant: x-axis = self-estimated number correct, y-axis = actual number correct; the dashed line is the 45° perfect-calibration line. Points below the line indicate overestimation.
